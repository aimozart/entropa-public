Glossary

Every term used on this site and in Entropa's product, explained in plain English. No background in AI, blockchains, or cryptography assumed — if a word here confuses you, that's a gap in this page, not in you.

The basics
AI agent
A piece of software that can make decisions on its own — reading information, deciding what to do, and taking an action — without a human clicking a button for every single step.
Probe — Entropa's name for an AI agent
Entropa's term for an AI agent that participates in the network. A Probe reasons about what to record (using an AI model), then cryptographically signs its decision before it's added to the chain. Think of it as "the AI whose actions we're keeping an honest record of."
Node
The running piece of software that keeps the record up to date — it collects new entries, puts them in order, and stores them. You can think of a node as the "engine" running behind the scenes.
Attestation
One recorded, signed, priced fact — "this AI agent did X, at this time, and here's cryptographic proof of it." This is the actual product: $0.01 buys one attestation.
Attestation Receipt
The proof document you get back for a specific attestation — everything you or an auditor need to independently confirm it's real and unaltered, explained both in plain English and in full technical detail. Look one up any time at /api/receipt/<id>.
The record itself
Chain (also: blockchain)
An ordered, growing list of records where each new entry is mathematically linked to the one before it. That link is what makes it "tamper-evident" — changing anything in an old entry would break every link after it, which is instantly detectable.
Block
One entry in that list — a bundle of one or more actions recorded together, along with the proof details (timestamp, fingerprint, signature) that make it verifiable.
Transaction
One specific recorded action inside a block — who did it, what kind of action it was, and the details of what happened.
Hash (also: fingerprint, digest)
A short string of letters and numbers computed from a piece of data, unique to that exact data. Change even one character of the original data, and the hash comes out completely different — so a matching hash proves nothing was altered.
Technical: Entropa uses BLAKE3, a fast, modern cryptographic hash function.
Signature (digital signature)
Cryptographic proof of exactly who created a piece of data — like a handwritten signature, but mathematically impossible to forge without the signer's private key.
Technical: Entropa uses ML-DSA (FIPS-204), a NIST-standardized post-quantum signature scheme — see below.
Why "post-quantum"
Post-quantum cryptography
Most encryption and digital signatures used online today could, in theory, eventually be broken by a sufficiently powerful quantum computer — a type of computer that doesn't exist at large scale yet, but is being actively developed. "Post-quantum" means a method designed to stay secure even after that happens.
ML-DSA / FIPS-204
The specific post-quantum digital signature standard Entropa uses, finalized by NIST (the US National Institute of Standards and Technology) in 2024. Every block on Entropa is signed this way.
How ordering is decided
Consensus
The process by which a network agrees on the order that entries get added, especially important if more than one participant could try to add something at the same time.
Proof of Entropy (PoE)
Entropa's own consensus method. Unlike "mining" (burning electricity to win the right to add the next entry) or "staking" (buying your way in with money), PoE picks who goes next using a public, independently-run random number source that nobody — including Entropa — can predict or control in advance.
Beacon (also: drand)
The public randomness source PoE relies on. "drand" is an existing, independent, real-world service that regularly publishes verifiable random values — Entropa reads from it rather than generating its own randomness, so nobody can secretly rig the outcome.
Mempool
A short-term waiting area for actions that have been submitted but not yet bundled into a block — typically a matter of seconds.
Reliability & storage
Epoch
An isolated storage generation for the chain's history. Each epoch's data lives in its own permanently separate location, so one epoch's records can never be overwritten by another's — see the Durability section for why this matters.
Using the product
API key
A private credential that identifies you as a specific customer when you submit an action to Entropa — like a password, but scoped specifically to this one purpose.
Scryon
Entropa's block explorer — a website (scryon.entropa.space) where you can browse every block and transaction ever recorded, in a searchable, human-readable form.
Compliance terms you'll see
HIPAA
A US law governing how healthcare information must be protected and handled.
HITRUST
A widely-recognized security certification, common in healthcare, that proves an organization meets a rigorous, independently-audited set of data-protection controls.
SOC 2
An independent audit report confirming a company has real, working controls around security, availability, and confidentiality of the systems it runs.
EU AI Act
European Union law regulating AI systems, with extra requirements for "high-risk" AI — including, under Article 12, automatic and tamper-resistant logging of what the AI system did.
NIST AI RMF
The US National Institute of Standards and Technology's voluntary framework for managing AI risk — it calls for documented, verifiable records of what an AI system actually did.
